01 / The idea

When communities thrive,
business can, too.

Every profession holds something useful: knowledge, relationships, resources, and the ability to get things done. Put those strengths in service of a community and their value grows.

That belief has shaped Alejandra Guzman’s work across business, economic development, and philanthropy. This space is an invitation to put it into practice together.

02 / The connection

Business is part of the community it serves.

Housing, work, health, and opportunity shape one another. Leaders can see those connections and choose to act on them.

Monterrey, Mexico
Where the idea began

A business belongs
to a place.

Growing up in Monterrey, Alejandra saw a model in which businesses invested in workers' health, education, and opportunity. The lesson stayed with her: a company and its community become stronger together.

The question now is what that kind of responsibility can look like here, and who is willing to help build it.

03 / The person behind the idea
Alejandra Guzman · New Orleans

Alejandra
Guzman.

For Alejandra, professional success means more when it creates opportunity beyond the walls of an organization.

She serves as Chief Operating Officer of LSU Health Foundation New Orleans. Across more than 20 years, her work has moved through large-scale programs, real estate, economic revitalization, and cross-sector partnerships. That experience includes roles at CEMEX and the New Orleans Business Alliance. She also serves with ULI Louisiana and the Hispanic Chamber of Commerce of Louisiana.

Raised in Monterrey, Mexico, Alejandra brings that formative perspective to the city she now calls home. She believes strategy matters most when it responds to community needs and creates room for others to lead.

04 / From experience to impact

Good intentions
need a plan.

Empathy tells us whom to serve. Strategy and careful execution make that service dependable. Alejandra has carried this conviction between private enterprise and civic institutions.

01

Housing with dignity.

At CEMEX, Alejandra worked on programs connecting families with building guidance, materials, and pathways to better housing.

02

A city, connected.

At the New Orleans Business Alliance, she worked across real estate, policy, and partnerships in communities affected by disinvestment.

03

Mission in practice.

As COO of LSU Health Foundation, she oversees operations and helps advance partnerships supporting health education and research.
